Clownfish live in sea anemones and are found throughout the Great Barrier Reef's shallow waters.

Brain corals are massive, slow-growing corals that can live for hundreds of years.

Green sea turtles are commonly seen grazing on seagrass in the reef flats.

Sunlight penetrates the clear water up to 30 meters deep in the reef flats.

Anemone colonies provide shelter for clownfish and other small reef fish.

Parrotfish use their beak-like teeth to scrape algae from coral, creating sand.

Giant clams can grow up to 1.2 meters wide in the shallow reef areas.

The reef flats are home to over 400 species of hard and soft corals.

Manta rays visit the reef flats during the summer months to feed on plankton.
